User Guide — IP Internal Management Software

A simple guide that explains, in plain language, how to use every part of the software: login, dashboard, tickets, daily tasks, payments, training, leads, and more. No technical knowledge needed.

Using this guide on a phone

On a phone or narrow screen, the left Contents list is tucked away so the page is readable. Use the bar at the top of the page to open it again.

This applies to this HTML User Guide (and the technical documentation file). The live website also has phone navigation: use ☰ at the top and the bottom tabs (Dashboard / Tickets / Tasks / More) after you log in — see Understanding the screen.

What was wrong before

  • On small screens the Contents sidebar disappeared completely.
  • There was no button to show it again, so you could only scroll — not jump between sections.

How to use Contents on a phone

  1. Open this file on your phone.
  2. Tap ☰ Menu at the top.
  3. Choose a section (for example 5. Support tickets).
  4. The page jumps to that section and the menu closes. Tap Menu again anytime.
You can also close the menu with the × button, by tapping the dimmed area outside the list, or by pressing Escape on a keyboard.

What does not change

  • On a wide screen (laptop/desktop) you still see the left Contents sidebar as before.
  • Printing or “Save as PDF” still hides the Contents list so the pages stay clean.

1. What is this software?

IP Internal Management Software is the one website where all Industry Prime team members do their daily work. Instead of many spreadsheets and WhatsApp messages, everything is in one place:

  • Support — report and track client problems and feature requests (tickets).
  • Task — your daily checklist and tasks given to you by others.
  • Onboarding & Payment — track new clients, invoices, and pending payments.
  • Training — track which client has finished which training step.
  • Success — monitor how clients are using the product and follow up with them.
  • Leads — track potential new clients from first contact to closing.
  • Dashboard & KPI — see your work summary and performance scores.

You open it in your web browser at: https://industryprime.vercel.app

You will only see the menus that are related to your job. If a colleague sees a menu that you don't see, it just means they have permission for that section and you don't. Your admin can give you access.

2. Getting started — register and login

First time? Create your account

  1. Open https://industryprime.vercel.app in Chrome or Edge.
  2. Click Register and fill in your name, work email, and a password.
  3. Open your email inbox — you will get a confirmation email. Click the link inside it.
  4. After confirming, come back to the website and login with your email and password.
If the confirmation email does not arrive, check your Spam folder. You can also click "Resend confirmation" on the login page.

Forgot your password?

  1. On the login page, click Forgot Password.
  2. Enter your email — you will get a reset link by email.
  3. Click the link and type your new password.

Loading — what you see while data loads

When you open a page or refresh, the software fetches your data from the server. Instead of a blank screen or a small spinning circle in the middle, you will see grey placeholder shapes that match what is coming — card blocks, table rows, or chart areas.

Why this matters

  • The layout stays stable — nothing jumps when numbers and rows appear.
  • The page feels faster even when the actual load time is the same.
  • You can tell what kind of content is loading (dashboard cards vs a long table).

What you will see

WhereLoading appearance
DashboardSoft grey tiles where operation counts and KPI cards will appear, then real numbers fade in.
Ticket / task listsTable-shaped grey rows (header + several lines) until your tickets or tasks load.
KPI dashboardCard grid + chart block placeholders; charts load after the main numbers.
Opening a ticket drawerShort grey lines inside the panel until ticket details load.
If placeholders stay for more than ~10 seconds on a slow connection, wait or refresh once. First load of the day may be slower while the server wakes up — see Common problems.
Repeat visits are faster: the Dashboard often shows cached summary data immediately, then quietly updates in the background.

Empty lists — no rows yet or no matches

When a table has nothing to show, you should see a clear message and often a button — not a tiny “No data” line with no explanation.

Two different situations

SituationWhat it meansWhat to do
Nothing here yetYou (or your team) have not created any records in this section.Use the suggested action — e.g. Submit Support Ticket, Add Task, Add Lead Details.
Filters hid everythingRecords exist, but your search, date range, or filters exclude all rows.Tap Clear filters or widen the date range, then try again.
No permissionThis section is not enabled for your account.Ask your admin under Users → permissions — not a bug.

Examples by section

  • Support tickets — “No open tickets matching this filter” vs “No tickets yet” with a path to create one.
  • Checklist / Delegation — “No tasks for Today” vs “No tasks match your search” with Add Task.
  • Leads — “No open leads” with Add Lead Details, or filter-empty with Clear filters.
  • Performance Monitoring — “No active companies” with guidance to add POC details (already detailed on that page).
  • KPI dashboard — “No KPI dashboard assigned” — admin must grant KPI access or assign a person dashboard.
If you expect data but see an empty list, check the filter chips and date picker at the top of the page before reporting a problem.

4. Dashboard — your daily starting point

Click Dashboard in the sidebar. This is your home page. It shows:

  • Today's snapshot — what needs your attention today.
  • My work — your open tickets, pending checklist items, and delegated tasks.
  • KPI overview — your performance scores (see section 12).
  • Operations tiles — quick numbers from Support, Success, Onboarding, Payment, and Training (you only see the tiles you have permission for).
  • Attendance & leave summary — attendance information pulled in automatically.
Start every morning from the Dashboard — it tells you what is pending before anyone has to remind you.

5. Support tickets — reporting problems and requests

A ticket is one record of a client problem or request. There are three types:

TypeMeaningReference number looks like
ChoreA small routine job (data change, setting, small help)EX-CH-0001
BugSomething in the product is broken or wrongEX-BU-0001
FeatureA request for something new to be builtEX-FE-0001

How to create a ticket

  1. Go to Operations → Support → Chores & Bugs (or Feature).
  2. Click the button to create a new ticket. A form opens.
  3. Fill in: company, which page/part of the product, title, and a clear description. Choose the type (chore / bug / feature) and priority.
  4. If you have a screenshot or file, attach it — a picture makes solving much faster.
  5. Submit. The system gives it a reference number automatically (like EX-BU-0042).
If you close the form halfway, don't worry — the software saves your draft automatically. When you open the form again, your text is still there.
When you type a ticket, the system also checks if a similar ticket already exists, so the same problem is not reported twice.

What happens to a ticket after you create it (the 4 stages)

Stage 1Ticket received and checked
→
Stage 2Work planned & in progress (remarks can be added)
→
Stage 3Solution ready, testing on staging
→
Stage 4Live for the client + quality review

Each stage has a planned date and an actual date. If actual is later than planned, the ticket shows a delay — this is how the SLA (service time promise) is tracked.

Feature approval (only for Feature tickets)

New features need approval before work starts. The approver gets an email with Approve / Reject / Hold buttons — they can click directly from the email, no login needed. You can see the current status under Support → Approval Status.

Other things you can do

  • Responses — write updates/questions on a ticket so everyone sees the history.
  • Stage-2 remarks — special remarks during work; the assigned person gets a 🔔 bell notification.
  • Promote to feature — if a chore/bug turns out to be a new feature request, it can be converted.
  • Register of Tickets — the full list of all tickets, with filters, for review and reporting.

6. Task — your Checklist and Delegation

Checklist (your repeating daily/weekly duties)

Go to Task → Checklist. This shows the duties assigned to you that repeat — daily, weekly, or monthly.

  1. Open the Checklist page every day.
  2. Do the task in real life, then click Complete next to it.
  3. If a task is genuinely not applicable today (for example, no data came), mark it NA instead.
Your checklist completion percentage goes into your KPI score. Uncompleted tasks lower your score, and you will get a reminder email every working day for pending items. Holidays are excluded automatically.

Delegation (one-time tasks given to you or by you)

Go to Task → Delegation. Here you see one-time tasks that someone assigned to you (or that you assigned to others), each with a due date. Complete them the same way. Pending delegation tasks also trigger daily reminder emails and affect KPI.

7. Onboarding — bringing a new client in

Go to Operations → Onboarding → Record of Onboarding. Each row is one new client. The flow is:

1. Payment statusRecord the client and their payment status
→
2. Pre-onboardingComplete the pre-onboarding checklist
→
3. POC & setupPOC checklist, organisation master, item cleaning
→
4. HandoverClient moves to Training and then Success

Click on a row to open its details and tick off each checklist item as you finish it. Everyone can see exactly how far each client's onboarding has reached.

8. Client Payment — invoices and follow-ups

Go to Operations → Client Payment → Payment Management.

The payment cycle

Raise invoiceAdd the invoice with amount and date
→
Mark as sentRecord when/how the invoice was sent to the client
→
Follow-upsRecord each reminder call/mail (up to 10 follow-ups)
→
Payment receivedRecord the payment — done!
  • Intercept — if something special happens (client dispute, on hold), tag it so the team knows.
  • Discontinuation — if the client stops the service, record it here.
  • Payment Ageing report — shows all unpaid invoices grouped by how old they are (e.g. 30 / 60 / 90+ days). Use this to see who to chase first.
  • Pending Payment Details — a special summary page (only visible to specific people).
You can also record quick payment actions directly from the Dashboard without opening the full payment page.

9. Training — teaching clients to use the product

Go to Operations → Training → Client Training. Each client has a training plan made of stages:

  1. The client is assigned to a trainer.
  2. Complete the Day-0 checklist (initial setup and introduction).
  3. Mark each training stage as done when the session is finished.

This way, anyone can see at a glance which client has finished which training step, and nothing gets skipped.

10. Success — keeping clients happy after training

Go to Operations → Success → Performance Monitoring. This page lists all live clients and how well they are using the product.

  • Performance grid — one row per company, showing their usage/performance.
  • Follow-ups — record every follow-up call you make to a client. The system tracks clicks and dates, so follow-ups are never forgotten.
  • POC — record the client's point-of-contact details.
  • Mark NA — if a company should not be counted (e.g. discontinued), mark it NA so reports stay clean.
  • Comp-Perform — a comparison view of company performance.

11. Client to Lead — tracking potential customers

Go to Operations → Client to Lead → Lead.

  1. Click to add a new lead, or use Import from sheet to bring many leads in at once from a spreadsheet.
  2. Open a lead to fill in its stage details — lead details, contacted, and the following stages as the conversation progresses.
  3. When the deal is finished (won or lost), the lead moves to Closed Leads.

Every lead keeps its full history, so anyone in the team can pick up the conversation.

12. KPI & scores — how your performance is measured

Go to Dashboard → KPI (or the Success dashboard, depending on your role). KPI = Key Performance Indicator = your work score.

What is countedWhere it comes from
Checklist completion %How many of your daily checklist items you completed on time
Delegation completion %How many delegated tasks you finished by the due date
Support / FMS %How fast tickets assigned to you moved through their stages (SLA)
Daily work logDaily scores entered for your role (some team members enter a short daily score)

You can view your score per week or per month, and click any number to see the detail behind it. Scores update automatically as soon as you complete work — no manual reporting needed.

Each person sees their own KPI board. Managers/admins can view team members' boards. Some team members (e.g. for social media or SLA tracking) have special boards designed for their role.

13. Emails the software will send you

The software sends automatic emails so nothing is forgotten. These are normal — you don't need to reply to them, just act on them:

EmailWhen you get itWhat to do
Checklist reminderEvery working day, if you have pending checklist itemsOpen the Checklist page and complete/NA the items
Delegation reminderEvery working day, if you have pending delegated tasksFinish the task and mark it complete
Pending digestPeriodic summary of everything pending for youReview and clear your pending list
Feature approval requestWhen a feature ticket needs your approvalClick Approve / Reject / Hold directly in the email
Escalation emailWhen a ticket is delayed too long or is criticalPrioritise that ticket immediately
Reminder emails are not sent on holidays. If you keep getting a reminder for a task that does not apply, mark that task NA in the software instead of ignoring the email.

14. For admins only

If you are an admin or master admin, you have two extra menus:

Users

  1. Go to Users in the sidebar.
  2. Click a user to edit them. Set their role (User / Approver / Admin / Master Admin).
  3. In the permission matrix, tick View and/or Edit for each section the user should access (Dashboard, Tickets, Client Payment, Training, etc.).
  4. Save. The user sees the new menus the next time they load the app.
If a user says "I can't see the XYZ menu", this permission matrix is the place to fix it. Give access here — do not ask for email-based exceptions.

Settings

  • Email settings — who receives feature-approval reminders and escalation emails, and when.
  • System lock — temporarily lock the whole system to read-only during maintenance (every lock/unlock is recorded).
